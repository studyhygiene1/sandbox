A Systematic Approach to Test Preparation

Anxious about the SAT, ACT, ASVAB, or GRE — or struggling in math, writing, or science? I work with students who want real improvement, not just shortcuts or quick tricks.

I'm a full-time tutor with a background in engineering and law — an M.Sc. in Electrical Engineering, a B.Sc. in Computer Engineering, and a J.D. with active legal licensure. I spent my early education in the CBSE system in India, where I was the top regional scorer in my city on board examinations, before earning a scholarship to study engineering at the University of Arizona following strong academic performance in the UK system at Loughborough University. Studying and working across the Indian, British, and American educational systems has given me a broad perspective on how different students are taught, and where they often get stuck.

I know what it's like to struggle, too. I was a C-minus student in math for most of my life — it was the one subject that never came easily, no matter how hard I tried elsewhere. In one intensive stretch, I studied math three hours a day, six days a week, for a month. On a grading scale where 75% was considered an A, I scored 100% on my final exam. That experience taught me that being "bad at math" is almost never really true — it usually means the material hasn't been explained in a way that connects yet.

That's the belief my tutoring is built on. Most students don't struggle because they don't get it — they struggle because something in how the material is being presented isn't connecting. My job is to find that gap quickly and fix it. I don't use a one-size-fits-all approach; I adjust to the student, not the other way around.

Over the years I've worked with students across math, physics, and standardized test prep — from SAT and ACT to ASVAB, GRE, GED, and college admissions essays. I also regularly help law students with IRAC structure, legal writing, and exam preparation, drawing on my own background in law school advocacy and mock trial. I've won public speaking competitions from elementary school through law school, at the school, state, regional, and national levels — that background shapes how I teach writing and essays, with a focus on clarity, structure, and persuasion, not just correctness.

But what students and parents tell me they appreciate most is simpler than any of that: I'm patient, I adapt to how each student learns, and I genuinely care whether they succeed. Many of my students come in frustrated or discouraged after struggling elsewhere — my goal is to help them rebuild both understanding and confidence.
